Xledger vs Visma for Norwegian Groups

A practical comparison for deal teams looking at Norwegian group companies — what each ledger gives you, what each leaves you to reconstruct, and what it means for the due diligence review.

Discuss a deal How we handle Norwegian ledgers

Why this question keeps coming up

Norwegian deal teams comparing Xledger and Visma for a group company are usually not comparing two accounting systems in the abstract. They are usually deciding between two ways of running a group — one centralised and one modular — and the choice has consequences that show up most clearly when a buyer tries to read the books.

For a financial due diligence review, the question is not which system is better in the abstract. It is: what does the system produce when you ask it for a SAF-T Financial export, and how much of the group structure is captured in the ledger versus reconstructed later? That is what determines whether the diligence review starts from source or from a summary.

The two systems at a glance

Both systems support SAF-T. Both can run a Norwegian group. The difference is in how the group is structured and how complete the export is.

Visma

The dominant mid-market choice across Norway — broad presence in SMEs and mid-market companies, with modules for accounts, payroll, CRM, e-invoicing, budget and project accounting spread across several Visma brands. For a due diligence review, the question is less about Visma the vendor than about which Visma subsystems the group actually runs and where the consolidation sits.

Xledger

An enterprise-grade ledger with deep multi-entity consolidation, granular permissions, and more customisation. Used by larger Norwegian groups that need to manage a complex structure. For diligence, the group accounting layer is usually in Xledger itself, which is an advantage — but the system is less familiar to the mid-market deal teams that usually need to read it.

What both produce

Both support SAF-T Financial v1.30. Both can export the full general ledger with master files and entries. For a due diligence review, that is the output that matters — the system brand is secondary to whether the export is complete, current and covers all group companies.

Where the difference actually bites a deal team

For a buyer doing due diligence on a Norwegian group, the real differences between Xledger and Visma show up in three places: how the group is consolidated, how consistent the accounting is across subsidiaries, and how easy it is to get the right export for every entity.

Group consolidation

Visma is a broad ecosystem; a Norwegian group can run several Visma products for different entities or different functions and still look like a Visma house from the outside. That means consolidation may sit in Visma, in a separate Visma product, or in Excel — and the deal team has to find out which. Xledger is more likely to have the consolidation built into the ledger itself, which is an advantage for a buyer: the group view is in the system, not in a smattering of linked spreadsheets.

Multi-entity consistency

Both systems can run groups consistently. The difference is enforcement. A group on Xledger is more likely to have centralised chart of accounts and policy settings across entities — which helps the buyer read the group the same way everywhere. A group on Visma is more likely to have each entity running its own module with its own setup — which is fine operationally but requires more legwork in diligence to confirm the accounting is applied the same way everywhere.

Export completeness

SAF-T Financial gives the buyer what a summary never could: full transaction-level detail. Both systems can produce it. The question for a deal team is whether the group's export covers every entity, whether the master files are consistent across entities, and whether the entity structure is documented well enough that the buyer can trace group-level figures back to source. That question has nothing to do with which system the group chose — it has everything to do with how well the group is set up.

What we recommend to deal teams

Do not let the system label drive the diligence strategy. Ask for the SAF-T export from every group entity, confirm the export is complete, and test the consolidation before you commit.

  • Send a sample or anonymised SAF-T export before you commit. We will return the derived monthly P&L, balance sheet and an account-level cut — before any engagement letter, at no cost.
  • Ask whether consolidation is in the ledger or in spreadsheets. A group where the consolidation is in Excel is not a red flag — it just tells you where the diligence effort goes. Find out early.
  • Check chart of accounts consistency across group entities. Where subsidiaries use different charts, the buyer's bridge to group-level figures takes longer. Where they share a chart, the review is faster.
  • Ask about the accounting policies for group-level items. Goodwill accounting, intercompany eliminations, FX translation, lease capitalisation under IFRS 16 — each of these is applied at the group level and each can diverge from the statutory accounts. Ask for the policy before you build the bridge.
  • Do not assume the system tells you the accounting. Xledger and Visma both let groups configure their own treatments. The system is a data source, not an accounting memo. Read the policies; do not infer them from the product name.

The short answer

If you are a Norwegian group choosing between the two, Visma is the safer operational choice for a mid-market group: broad support, broad familiarity, clean SAF-T exports, and enough consolidation capability for most mid-market structures. Xledger is the better choice for a larger group that needs deeper multi-entity consolidation, more granular permissions, or a more centralised group accounting layer — but it comes with a steeper learning curve for the deal teams that read the books later.

For a financial due diligence review, the system name matters far less than the data. A Visma house with a clean centralised export and a consistent chart of accounts is fast and predictable. A Visma house where each subsidiary runs its own module and the consolidation is in Excel is slower and less predictable. The same is true of Xledger — the system is not the bottleneck, the group setup is.

Frequently asked questions

Is Xledger or Visma better for a Norwegian group company?

It depends on the group structure. Visma is the dominant mid-market choice in Norway with broad presence across subsidiaries and clean SAF-T exports. Xledger is used by larger groups that need deeper multi-entity consolidation and more granular permissions. For a financial due diligence review, the deal team cares about which system produces a usable SAF-T Financial export and whether the consolidation layer sits in the same system or in separate tools.

What matters most for financial due diligence?

The SAF-T Financial export. Whether the group runs Xledger, Visma, Tripletex or PowerOffice Go, the review starts from whatever XML the ledger produces natively. The question for a deal team is not which system is better in the abstract — it is whether the export gives full transaction-level detail, whether the multi-entity consolidation is captured in ledgers or in spreadsheets, and whether the accounting policies are applied consistently across group companies.

What is the weakness of each system for diligence?

Visma's weakness for groups is that it is a broad ecosystem of modules and brands; a group can look like a Visma house without any single system giving a complete picture, and consolidation may be spread across several products or in Excel. Xledger's weakness is that it is less familiar to the mid-market deal teams that usually need it — fewer accountants have hands-on experience — and groups using Xledger are more likely to have run their own customisation, which needs extra care in review.

Does the ledger choice affect the speed of a due diligence review?

Not directly. Speed depends on the quality and completeness of the SAF-T export and on the consistency of the group accounting policies. A Visma house with a clean centralised export is fast. A Visma house where each subsidiary runs its own module and consolidation is in Excel is not. The same applies to Xledger. The system is not the bottleneck — the data model and the group setup are.
