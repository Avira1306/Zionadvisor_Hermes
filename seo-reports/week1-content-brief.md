## Structure

### Lead (150 words)
A foreign PE fund or strategic buyer commissions diligence on a Swedish target. The data room arrives. The local advisor builds a trial balance from whatever Excel export the seller provided. Two weeks in, nobody has opened the SIE file sitting in the accounting system. This is the most common starting mistake on Swedish deals — and it costs time, budget, and sometimes the deal.

### H2: Why Sweden is different from UK/US diligence
- SIE file as the primary data source, not the management pack
- K2 vs K3 accounting regimes — what the buyer must bridge
- The role of the statutory annual report (arsredovisning) vs management accounts
- How offshore teams without Swedish accounting knowledge misprice the engagement

### H2: The SIE4 file: what it is and why you should ask for it first
- SIE4 carries full transactions and vouchers — a complete audit trail in one file
- Every Swedish accounting system (Fortnox, Visma, Bokio,/redovisningssystems) produces it
- Why building a databook from SIE4 is faster than from re-keyed spreadsheets
- SIE5 (XML) vs SIE4 — which one you get in practice

### H2: K2 vs K3: where EBITDA diverges
- Capitalised development costs under K2
- Goodwill amortisation differences
- Operating lease treatment (IFRS 16 vs K2/K3)
- What the buyer's underwriting basis actually requires
- How Zion Advisor bridges K2 numbers onto the buyer's basis and shows the bridge

### H2: Working capital pegs in Swedish deals
- Seasonality: construction (summer), retail (Q4), tourism (Q2)
- Why the 12-month average is wrong for a peg
- What the peg should reflect: the point in the cycle at completion
- Red flags in NWC that Swedish targets commonly carry

### H2: Red flags foreign buyers miss in Swedish FDD
- Related-party transactions buried in statutory notes
- Founder-owned premises at non-market rent
- Customer concentration without churn analysis
- Deferred tax not modelled
- The SIE file that doesn't reconcile to statutory accounts

### H2: How to commission FDD on a Swedish target
- What to ask for in the data request (SIE4, statutory accounts, management pack)
- When to bring in a specialist vs generalist offshore team
- Fixed-project vs retainer: which makes sense for Swedish deals
- Typical timeline: first-cut databook in 48–72 hours
