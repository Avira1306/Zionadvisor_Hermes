What we do on a Norwegian mandate

SAF-T — the file that drives the engagement

SAF-T (Standard Audit File for Tax) is Norway's mandated standard audit file. Version 1.30 became required for accounting periods beginning January 2025.

What SAF-T contains

An XML document structured as Header, Master Files and General Ledger Entries — giving full transaction-level detail. The same completeness that SIE4 gives us on a Swedish target.

Why it matters

Because SAF-T is mandatory, transaction-level data is reliably available on Norwegian targets. This shortens the reconstruction phase and more of the budget goes to analysis.

What we ask for

The SAF-T Financial export first. It gives the same transaction-level completeness that SIE4 gives us on a Swedish target, so the analysis starts from source rather than from a summary.

NGAAP and IFRS — where the differences bite

A buyer underwriting on an IFRS basis cannot read NGAAP numbers at face value. These are the divergences that actually move EBITDA and net debt.

Which standard applies

Most Norwegian companies report under NGAAP (Norwegian GAAP). Listed groups and some larger entities use IFRS.

Goodwill

NGAAP requires goodwill to be amortised over its useful life. Under IFRS, it is tested for impairment instead. The amortisation charge is normally added back for an IFRS-basis buyer.

Capitalised costs

Development cost capitalisation rules differ. Where a software or product target has capitalised costs under one framework but not another, reported EBITDA shifts materially.

Leases

Operating leases sit off balance sheet under NGAAP. A buyer applying IFRS 16 would capitalise them, affecting both EBITDA and net debt.

Why it matters in diligence

We bridge reported NGAAP figures onto the basis the buyer is underwriting, and show the bridge so a buyer can see how the number was built.

Statutory filing

Norwegian limited companies file an årsregnskap with Brønnøysundregistrene. We reconcile those filed statutory accounts back to management reporting as a standard early step, because that reconciliation is almost always where a buyer's first substantive questions land — particularly where the statutory accounts are prepared on a different basis or with different cut-offs from the monthly management pack.

The Norwegian M&A market

  • Norway's PE market is concentrated in oil and gas, energy transition, maritime, seafood and technology.
  • The Norwegian Sovereign Wealth Fund (NBIM) sets a benchmark for governance and ESG standards across the market.
  • Oslo hosts mid-market houses including FSN Capital, Reitan & Co, Norvestor and Vizore, alongside the regional offices of EQT and Nordic Capital.
  • Energy transition and decarbonisation are creating a growing pipeline of lower-mid-market transaction flow, which is exactly where outsourced diligence is common.
  • A deep base of owner-led businesses reaching succession means consistent deal flow where in-house diligence teams do not exist.

Language and currency

  • Norwegian source data. Account names, voucher narrative and supporting documents are processed in Norwegian. Nothing needs translating before it reaches us.
  • English deliverables. Reports and databooks are issued in English by default, or bilingual where the seller's team needs to review them too.
  • NOK reporting. We handle NOK-reported entities with EUR or USD reporting lines, including translation effects on comparatives.
  • FX in the peg. Where working capital is NOK-denominated but the peg is agreed in EUR or USD, we show the translation effect separately rather than burying it.

What is SAF-T and why does it matter in Norway?

It is an XML document structured as Header, Master Files and General Ledger Entries, giving full transaction-level detail. This means analysis can start from source rather than a summary.
