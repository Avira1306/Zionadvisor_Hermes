NGAAP and IFRS — where the differences bite

A buyer underwriting on an IFRS basis cannot read NGAAP numbers at face value. These are the divergences that actually move EBITDA and net debt.

Which standard applies

Most Norwegian companies report under NGAAP (Norwegian GAAP). Listed groups and some larger entities use IFRS.

Goodwill

NGAAP requires goodwill to be amortised over its useful life. Under IFRS, it is tested for impairment instead. The amortisation charge is normally added back for an IFRS-basis buyer.

Capitalised costs

Development cost capitalisation rules differ. Where a software or product target has capitalised costs under one framework but not another, reported EBITDA shifts materially.

Leases

Operating leases sit off balance sheet under NGAAP. A buyer applying IFRS 16 would capitalise them, affecting both EBITDA and net debt.

Why it matters in diligence

We bridge reported NGAAP figures onto the basis the buyer is underwriting, and show the bridge so a buyer can see how the number was built.

Ledger data and what we ask for

SAF-T Financial

Norway's mandated standard audit file. An XML document structured as Header, Master Files and General Ledger Entries — giving full transaction-level detail. Version 1.30 became required for accounting periods beginning January 2025.

Practical notes

  • NGAAP permits some treatments that diverge from IFRS — we bridge onto the buyer's basis rather than presenting NGAAP figures unqualified.
  • SAF-T being mandatory means transaction-level data is reliably available on Norwegian targets, which shortens the reconstruction phase.
  • CET working hours give full overlap with the Norwegian business day.

The Norwegian M&A market

Norway punches well above its weight in deal volume, and most of the flow is mid-market — exactly where in-house diligence teams do not exist.

  • The oil and gas supply chain, aquaculture, renewable energy, IT and maritime services drive most mid-market transaction flow.
  • Equinor and Equinor-linked families remain the largest pool of founder-family sellers, often selling minority stakes to financial or strategic buyers.
  • MEH, Verdane, Altor and EQT are the most active financial sponsors, alongside mid-market houses including IK Partners and Adelis.
  • Most targets are owner-led, often founder-family controlled, and usually have one or two entities in the group rather than a holding-company pyramid.

Red flags we look for

  • Related-party transactions. Founder-family controlled groups often transact from a holding structure, so we map related parties and unwind intra-group pricing before valuing the target.
  • Transfer pricing. Where a group has cross-border services, we test whether pricing reflects market terms or has been set to move profit between entities.
  • Revenue quality. We check whether reported revenue is recurring or lumpy, and whether customer concentration is high enough that one loss moves EBITDA materially.
  • Working capital seasonality. Norwegian targets in construction, retail and aquaculture carry seasonal working capital swings that an annual figure hides.
  • Statutory vs management cut-offs. We reconcile filed figures back to management reporting early, because that reconciliation is where the first hard questions land.

Frequently asked questions

What is transaction advisory?

Transaction advisory supports buyers and sellers in M&A deals. It includes financial due diligence, quality of earnings analysis, working capital pegs, databook preparation, and help with purchase price adjustments. The goal is to give the deal team confidence in the numbers before they commit.

Do you work from a SAF-T Financial file?

Yes — it is the first thing we ask for on a Norwegian target. SAF-T Financial carries transaction-level general ledger entries, so the monthly P&L, balance sheet and account detail all come from one structured export rather than a request the seller’s controller has to rebuild.

What changed with SAF-T v1.30?

Version 1.30 became the required format for accounting periods beginning 1 January 2025. It is an XML document structured as Header, Master Files and General Ledger Entries, and it is the version we read on current Norwegian engagements.

Which Norwegian ledger systems do you handle?

Tripletex, Visma, PowerOffice Go and Xledger cover most of the SME and mid-market segment. If the target runs something less common, send a sample export and we will confirm we can read it before you commit to anything.

How do you handle NGAAP versus IFRS?

We bridge reported NGAAP figures onto the basis you are underwriting and show the bridge rather than presenting a restated number without workings. Development costs, provisions and lease treatment are the divergences that most often move EBITDA.

Do you reconcile the statutory filing?

Yes. We reconcile the årsregnskap filed with Brønnøysundregistrene back to management reporting early in the engagement, because that reconciliation is reliably where a buyer’s first hard questions land.
