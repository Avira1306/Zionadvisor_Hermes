Reporting basis

Årsregnskabsloven (the Danish Financial Statements Act), which sets reporting class A–D requirements by company size; IFRS for listed groups.

We bridge reported local-GAAP figures onto the basis the buyer is underwriting, and show the bridge rather than presenting a restated number without workings.

Practical notes

  • Reporting class materially affects what is disclosed — a class B filing tells a buyer far less than a class C, so more of the work moves into diligence.
  • Danish filings are publicly accessible, which supports a useful pre-diligence read before data room access.
  • CET working hours give full overlap with the Danish business day.

The Danish M&A market

Denmark is the most concentrated Nordic market — a handful of large corporates dominate, but deal flow underneath them is consistent and active.

  • Novo Nordisk and the pharma/healthcare ecosystem, shipping (Maersk group), dairy/food (Arla, Danish Crown) and enterprise IT drive most mid-market interest.
  • CI Capital, Axcel, FSN Capital and Offspring Capital are the most active financial sponsors in the SME and mid-market space.
  • Most targets are ApS (limited liability) structures, often founder or family controlled, with lean management teams where one or two people carry multiple hats.
  • Because the market is small and well connected to Germany, cross-border targets with German operations, German-speaking ownership or German supply chains are common.
  • Denmark is in the euro, which removes the SEK, NOK and DKK translation layer present elsewhere in the Nordics.

How a Danish diligence engagement runs

Before we start

We read the publicly filed årsrapport first. Danish filings are accessible through Erhvervsstyrelsen, so we build a pre-diligence read before the data room even opens.

Reporting class shapes scope

A class B filing discloses far less than a class C. We read the class first and let it shape the data request rather than issuing a generic one.

Source data

e-conomic, Dinero, Visma and Microsoft Dynamics are the main ledger stacks. We take the general ledger export the system natively produces.

Deliverables

Within 48-72 hours we return the derived monthly P&L, balance sheet and a working-capital estimate, with a full databook to follow.

No minimum commitment. A first databook is a low-risk way to see the standard before a larger engagement.

Red flags we look for

  • Owner-manager salary optimization. Founder-led Danish ApS companies often pay themselves at a level that optimizes tax rather than reflects market salary — we normalize this before valuing the business.
  • Transfer pricing with Germany. Cross-border transactions with the closest large market need to be tested for arm's-length pricing.
  • Thin capitalization. Danish interest deduction rules (§ 4-3 of the Tax Act) can limit deductibility where debt is high relative to equity — we check the capital structure early.
  • Intangible asset valuation. Where a target's value sits in brands, software or customer relationships rather than tangible assets, we scrutinize the valuation assumptions carefully.
  • Revenue concentration. A Danish mid-market target with one or two large customers carries risk the headline EBITDA does not reflect.

Frequently asked questions

Which Danish reporting class applies to a target?

Danish companies report under classes A to D depending on size, as set by Årsregnskabsloven. The class determines disclosure depth — and therefore how much a buyer can learn from the filed accounts before diligence even starts.

How does the reporting class affect diligence scope?

A class B filing tells a buyer considerably less than a class C, so more of the analytical work moves into diligence. We read the filed accounts first and use that to shape the data request rather than issuing a generic list.

Which Danish ledger systems do you handle?

e-conomic and Dinero lead the SME segment, with Visma and Microsoft Dynamics appearing in the mid-market and above. We take the general ledger export the system natively produces.

Are Danish filings publicly available before diligence?
